# Iris's 12-Week Home Phonics Plan

A home phonics plan for a 6-year-old Thai learner of English. Each session takes 15 minutes, five days a week, and uses things you already have at home.

---

## How to Use This Plan

- **Schedule:** 15 minutes a day, Monday to Friday. Weekends are off, or you can do a 5-minute "sound hunt" on an outing if you like.
- **Best time:** When Iris has eaten and isn't tired. Late afternoon after school usually works better than bedtime.
- **The golden rule:** Say **pure sounds**. Say "sss" and "mmm", not "suh" and "muh". For stop sounds like /t/, /p/ and /k/, keep them short and crisp. Don't add "uh" or "er". Extra vowels make blending harder, and Thai learners already tend to add vowels between consonants.
- **Sound first, letter name later.** Teach "the letter that says /s/" before teaching that it's called "ess".
- **Speak Thai when it helps.** Explaining a mouth position in Thai is fine, and so is saying "เหมือนเสียงงูขู่" ("like a snake hissing"). Keep the target sound itself English.
- **Don't spell English sounds with Thai letters.** Writing "แคท" for "cat" is a habit to avoid. Thai has no final /t/ released the way English does, so Thai spelling quietly carries Thai pronunciation into English.
- **Praise effort, then fix one thing.** "Great reading! Let's make the end of 'cat' pop, /t/!" Correct only one sound per word.

### The Sequence and Why It Works

The order follows the widely used UK *Letters and Sounds* Phase 2 progression. It starts with **s, a, t, p**, then **i, n, m, d**, then **g, o, c, k**, then **ck, e, u, r**, then **h, b, f, ff, l, ll, ss** ([Letters and Sounds Phase 2](https://letters-and-sounds.com/phase-2-introduction/); [NSW Letters and Sounds guide](https://cer.schools.nsw.gov.au/content/dam/doe/sws/schools/c/cer/localcontent/letters_and_sounds-2.pdf)). After just the first six letters (s, a, t, p, i, n), Iris can already read many real words: sat, pin, tap, nap, tin, pit, ant.

I've adapted the order for a Thai speaker. Sounds that Thai lacks (/v/, /z/, /sh/, /th/, /j/, and hard voiced /g/) come later and get extra mouth practice. **Final consonants** get attention from Week 1, because they are the biggest Thai-to-English problem.

## The 12-Week Letter Sequence

| Week | New sounds | Example words Iris can read | Tricky words (learn by sight) | Thai-interference focus |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **s, a, t, p** | sat, tap, pat, at, as, sap | — | Final /t/ and /p/ must be **heard** (cat, not "ca'") |
| 2 | **i, n, m, d** | pin, tin, sit, man, mad, dad, dip, it, in | I | Final /d/ stays voiced (mad ≠ mat). Short /i/ stays short |
| 3 | **g, o, c, k** | dog, cot, got, cap, kit, pot, top, can | the, to | Voiced /g/ (Thai has no true /g/). Final /k/ is released |
| 4 | **ck, e, u, r** | sock, duck, peg, bed, red, rug, sun, cup | no, go | /e/ vs /a/ (bed/bad). /r/ vs /l/ |
| 5 | **h, b, f, l** (+ ff, ll, ss) | hat, big, bus, fan, fun, leg, hill, kiss, off | into, he, she | Final /l/ is **not** /n/ (ball ≠ "ban"). Final /s/ and /f/ |
| 6 | **Review week:** all 5 short vowels and CVC words | Mixed CVC words (e.g. "red bus", "big dog") | we, me, be | Ends of words: "sound sandwiches" |
| 7 | **j, v, w, x** | jam, jet, van, vet, web, wet, box, fox, six | was, my | /v/ is **not** /w/ (vet ≠ wet). Final /ks/ in "box" |
| 8 | **y, z, zz, qu** | yes, yet, zip, zap, buzz, fizz, quiz, quick | you, they | /z/ buzzes, not /s/ (zip ≠ sip). Voiced final /z/ |
| 9 | **sh, ch, th** | ship, shop, fish, chip, chin, much, thin, this, bath | all, are | /sh/ ≠ /ch/ (ship ≠ chip). /th/ is **not** /t/, /d/, or /s/ |
| 10 | **ng, nk**, plural **-s** | ring, sing, king, pink, bank, dogs, cats, hens | her, said | Don't drop plural /s/ or /z/ (cats, dogs) |
| 11 | **Starting blends:** st, sp, sn, sl, fl, pl, cl, fr, tr, gr | stop, spot, snap, flag, plum, clap, frog, trip, grin | have, like | No extra vowel: "stop", not "sa-top". Keep the /r/ in "frog" |
| 12 | **Ending blends** (-st, -nd, -mp, -nt, -lk) **+ final review** | fast, best, hand, sand, jump, lamp, tent, milk | some, come | Both final sounds: "hand", not "han". Final review |

## Daily 15-Minute Session Structure

Use the same structure every day so Iris knows what's coming.

| Minutes | Segment | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| 0–2 | **Warm-up flash** | Show the sound cards learned so far (Post-its or paper squares). Iris says the sound. Go fast and keep it fun. |
| 2–5 | **Mouth and sound** | Introduce or review the week's sound with a **mirror**: what lips, tongue and breath do. Add an action (see the action list below). |
| 5–10 | **Multisensory activity** | The day's hands-on activity (see the weekly plans). |
| 10–13 | **Blend and segment** | Read 3–5 words by blending (s-a-t → sat). Then segment: you say a word and Iris taps out each sound. |
| 13–15 | **Finish and celebrate** | One quick game, a sticker on the progress chart, and tick the checklist. |

### Weekly Rhythm (Same Every Week)

- **Monday: Meet the sound.** Introduce the new sounds with a story, an action and a mirror.
- **Tuesday: Touch the sound.** Tactile day: rice tray, playdough, writing on a back.
- **Wednesday: Build words.** Make and change words with letter caps or cards.
- **Thursday: Play with sounds.** Movement games: hopscotch, treasure hunt, fishing game.
- **Friday: Show me.** Review and a quick progress check (see the checklist), plus a tiny "reading show" for a family member.

## Week-by-Week Activity Plans

### Week 1: s, a, t, p

**Goal:** Recognize and say s, a, t, p. Blend "sat", "tap", "pat", "at".

| Day | Activity (5 minutes) | Items |
|---|---|---|
| Mon | **Sound hunt:** Walk around the house and find things that start with /s/ (sock, spoon, soap) and /p/ (pen, pot, pillow). Drop them in a basket. | Basket |
| Tue | **Rice tray writing:** Trace s, a, t, p in rice while saying the sound. Say the sound as you write, not after. | Tray + rice |
| Wed | **Bottle-cap words:** Line up caps s-a-t. Push each cap forward as you say its sound, then sweep a finger under all of them to blend: "sat". Swap s for p: "pat". | Letter caps |
| Thu | **Tissue test:** Hold a tissue in front of Iris's mouth. Say "tap" and "pat": the tissue should move on the /t/ and /p/. Make a game of "make it dance". | Tissue |
| Fri | **Show me:** Flash the cards and read 4 words. Tick the checklist. | Cards |

**Interference watch:** Thai words can end in an unreleased /t/ or /p/, so Iris may say "sa'" for "sat". Have her put a hand in front of her mouth and **feel** the final puff.

### Week 2: i, n, m, d

**Goal:** Read CVC words with a and i (pin, sit, man, mad, dip).

| Day | Activity | Items |
|---|---|---|
| Mon | **Mirror mouths:** Watch lips close for /m/ and stay open for /n/. Hum "mmm" while touching lips together. | Mirror |
| Tue | **Playdough letters:** Roll "snakes" and form i, n, m, d. Iris pokes the dot on the i. | Playdough |
| Wed | **Sound boxes:** Use an ice-cube tray. Say "pin". Iris pushes one bead or rice grain into each cup for /p/ /i/ /n/. | Ice tray + beads |
| Thu | **Mad or mat?** You say a word. Iris holds up a "d" card (throat buzz) or a "t" card (puff only). Touch the throat to feel /d/ buzz. | Cards |
| Fri | **Show me** + read "I sit", "I nap" using the tricky word "I". | Cards |

**Interference watch:** Final /d/ often becomes /t/ ("mad" sounds like "mat") because Thai has no voiced final stops. Use the throat-buzz check.

### Week 3: g, o, c, k

**Goal:** Read words with o (dog, pot, top, cot). Learn that c and k both say /k/.

| Day | Activity | Items |
|---|---|---|
| Mon | **Gulp game:** Sip water, then say /g/ like a gulp. Compare "goat" and "coat" with a hand on the throat. | Cup of water |
| Tue | **Back writing:** Trace a letter on Iris's back with your finger. She guesses and says the sound, then switches roles. | — |
| Wed | **Word ladder:** cot → cat → cap → tap → top → pot. Change one cap at a time. | Letter caps |
| Thu | **Fishing game:** Put paper-clip "fish" (word cards) in a bowl and fish with a magnet or chopsticks. Read each catch. | Paper, chopsticks |
| Fri | **Show me** + read "to the dog", "to the pot". | Cards |

**Interference watch:** Iris may say /g/ like Thai "ก" (unvoiced). Throat buzz again. For final /k/ in "sock" and "back", make it audible.

### Week 4: ck, e, u, r

**Goal:** All five short vowels except review. Read words with e and u.

| Day | Activity | Items |
|---|---|---|
| Mon | **Vowel family:** Make 5 paper "vowel houses" (a, e, i, o, u). Put toys in the right house by middle sound (cup → u, pen → e). | Paper, toys |
| Tue | **Salt tray + ck:** Write "duck", "sock", "kick". Explain that "ck" is two letters making one sound, like "twins". | Salt tray |
| Wed | **Bed or bad?** Hold up two cards. Say words. Iris points to the vowel she hears. Watch in the mirror: the mouth opens wider for /a/. | Mirror, cards |
| Thu | **Motorbike /r/:** Drive a toy car over r-word cards (red, rug, run) while revving "rrr". Then contrast "red" with "led". | Toy car |
| Fri | **Show me** + **Milestone 1 check** (see the checklist). | Checklist |

**Interference watch:** /e/ vs /a/ (bed/bad, pen/pan) is often confused. /r/ and /l/ can swap, and /r/ in Thai speech is often said as /l/.

### Week 5: h, b, f, l, ff, ll, ss

**Goal:** Read words with doubled endings. Say final /l/ and /f/ clearly.

| Day | Activity | Items |
|---|---|---|
| Mon | **Lollipop /l/:** Iris licks the ridge behind her top teeth with her tongue tip. Say "lll". Then say "ball" and **hold** the tongue up at the end. | Mirror |
| Tue | **Flashlight letters:** Turn off the lights, shine the torch on letter cards stuck around the room, and Iris says the sound. | Torch, Post-its |
| Wed | **Double trouble:** Build "hill", "bell", "kiss", "off". Explain that doubled letters at the end of short words make one sound. | Caps |
| Thu | **Bounce and say:** Bounce a ball once for each sound in a word: b-u-s (3 bounces), h-i-ll (3 bounces). | Ball |
| Fri | **Show me** + read "he has a big hat", "she is on a bus". | Cards |

**Interference watch:** Final /l/ often becomes /n/ ("ball" sounds like "ban", "bell" like "ben"), and "path" can become "pap" ([Memorable Press](https://memorablepress.com/thai-english-consonants/)). Final /f/ and /s/ are often dropped.

### Week 6: Review and "Sound Sandwiches"

**Goal:** Read any CVC word fluently. Make **every** final sound clearly.

| Day | Activity | Items |
|---|---|---|
| Mon | **Sound sandwich:** Use 3 bread-shaped paper pieces (top bread = first sound, filling = vowel, bottom bread = last sound). "No sandwich without the bottom bread." | Paper |
| Tue | **Rice tray dictation:** You say a word, and Iris writes it in rice. | Rice tray |
| Wed | **Silly sentences:** Build "a red bug in a hot pot" with cards, then act it out. | Cards |
| Thu | **Treasure hunt:** Hide 8 word cards around the room. Iris must read each one to "keep" it. | Cards |
| Fri | **Reading show:** Iris reads 10 words to a grandparent or relative (video call works too). | Phone |

### Week 7: j, v, w, x

**Goal:** Say /v/ and /w/ as two different sounds. Read "box", "fox", "six".

| Day | Activity | Items |
|---|---|---|
| Mon | **Vacuum vs wind:** /v/ = top teeth on bottom lip + buzz (lip tickles). /w/ = round lips like a kiss, no teeth. Check in the mirror. | Mirror |
| Tue | **Playdough j, v, w, x**, saying each sound while building. | Playdough |
| Wed | **Vet or wet?** Minimal pairs: vet/wet, van/wan, vest/west. Iris shows "teeth" (v) or "kiss" (w). | — |
| Thu | **Box game:** Put objects in a real box and say "a ___ in a box": a pen in a box, a sock in a box. Stress the /ks/ at the end. | Box, toys |
| Fri | **Show me** + read "my jam", "was in a box". | Cards |

**Interference watch:** /v/ tends to become /w/ at the start of words and /f/ or /p/ at the end ([JEL Journal, Thai EFL consonant study](https://so17.tci-thaijo.org/index.php/JEL/article/download/2385/1502)). Thai uses "ว" for English v in loanwords ([Memorable Press](https://memorablepress.com/thai-vs-english/)).

### Week 8: y, z, zz, qu

**Goal:** Make /z/ buzz. Read "buzz", "zip", "quiz".

| Day | Activity | Items |
|---|---|---|
| Mon | **Bee or snake?** /s/ = snake (no throat buzz). /z/ = bee (throat buzzes). Hand on throat. Iris "flies" for z and "slithers" for s. | — |
| Tue | **Back writing + flying finger:** Trace a z in the air like a bee's zig-zag path. | — |
| Wed | **Word builder:** zip, zap, buzz, fizz, yes, yet, quiz, quit. | Caps |
| Thu | **Sip or zip?** Minimal pairs: sip/zip, sue/zoo. Iris buzzes or hisses back. | — |
| Fri | **Show me** + **Milestone 2 check**. | Checklist |

**Interference watch:** /z/ is usually replaced with /s/. In one study of Thai learners, final /z/ was said correctly only 3.7% of the time ([Walailak AJAC study](https://ajaclibrary.wu.ac.th/index.php/ajac/article/download/77/34)). Don't expect perfection here. Aim for awareness.

### Week 9: sh, ch, th

**Goal:** Read words with digraphs and say /sh/ and /th/ correctly.

| Day | Activity | Items |
|---|---|---|
| Mon | **Shhh vs choo-choo:** /sh/ = long, smooth air (sleeping baby). /ch/ = short burst (train). Iris holds "shhh" for 3 seconds. | — |
| Tue | **Tongue peek /th/:** In the mirror, Iris puts her tongue tip between her teeth and blows. "If I can't see your tongue, it's not th." Do "thin" (no buzz) and "this" (buzz). | Mirror |
| Wed | **Sorting cups:** Three paper cups labeled sh, ch, th. Sort picture or word cards (fish, chip, bath, shop, chin, thin). | Cups, cards |
| Thu | **Fish shop role-play:** Play shop with toy "fish" and "chips". "Can I have a fish? A chip?" | Toys |
| Fri | **Show me** + read "all the fish are in the shop". | Cards |

**Interference watch:** /sh/ often becomes /ch/ ("ship" sounds like "chip") ([Walailak AJAC study](https://ajaclibrary.wu.ac.th/index.php/ajac/article/download/77/34)). /th/ becomes /t/, /d/ or /s/, and at the end of words often a cut-off /t/ ("with" sounds like "wit") ([JEL Journal](https://so17.tci-thaijo.org/index.php/JEL/article/download/2385/1502)).

### Week 10: ng, nk, Plural -s

**Goal:** Read "ring", "pink", and plurals. Keep the plural ending.

| Day | Activity | Items |
|---|---|---|
| Mon | **Ring a bell:** "nnng". Thai has this sound (ง), so make it a confidence win. Then "nk": pink, bank, sink. | Bell or spoon on a glass |
| Tue | **One or many?** Show 1 banana, then 3 bananas. Say "banana" and then "bananas", stressing the end. Hiss for /s/ (cats) and buzz for /z/ (dogs). | Fruit, toys |
| Wed | **Plural builder:** Add an "s" cap to the end of cat, dog, pen, hen. Read both forms. | Caps |
| Thu | **Counting hunt:** "Find 2 cups, 3 spoons, 4 pegs." Iris must say the plural ending to "win" the items. | Household items |
| Fri | **Show me** + read "she said the kings sing". | Cards |

**Interference watch:** Thai doesn't mark plurals on the word, and final /s/ is weak, so "two cat" is very common. Thai learners also tend to drop final suffix sounds generally ([SWU Journal](https://ejournals.swu.ac.th/index.php/hm/article/download/632/632)).

### Week 11: Starting Blends

**Goal:** Say two starting consonants **together**, with no vowel between them.

| Day | Activity | Items |
|---|---|---|
| Mon | **Glue sounds:** Hold "sss" and slide straight into "t": "sssstop". Use two laundry pegs clipped together to show the sounds are "stuck". | Pegs |
| Tue | **Playdough blends:** Make two letters, squish them together, and say the blend: st, sp, fl, cl. | Playdough |
| Wed | **Word builder:** snap, spot, stop, flag, clap, plum, frog, trip, grin. | Caps |
| Thu | **Hop the blend:** Lay blend cards on the floor. Iris hops onto one and reads a word starting with it. | Paper cards |
| Fri | **Show me** + read "I like to clap", "frogs have legs". | Cards |

**Interference watch:** Thai speakers often add a vowel inside clusters ("stop" becomes "sa-top" in Thai loanword pronunciation) or drop the /r/ or /l/ (ประตู can sound like "patuu") ([Memorable Press](https://memorablepress.com/thai-vs-english/)). Keep blends smooth, and check that the /r/ in "frog" and "trip" is there.

### Week 12: Ending Blends and Final Review

**Goal:** Say both final consonants. Celebrate 12 weeks of progress.

| Day | Activity | Items |
|---|---|---|
| Mon | **Two-tail words:** Draw a cat with two tails, one for each ending sound: ha-n-d, ju-m-p, fa-s-t. "Don't cut off a tail." | Paper, pen |
| Tue | **Sand writing:** Write hand, sand, jump, lamp, tent, milk. | Rice or salt tray |
| Wed | **Sound boxes (4 cups):** Segment "jump" (j-u-m-p) and "best" (b-e-s-t). | Egg carton |
| Thu | **Final treasure hunt:** 15 mixed word cards from all 12 weeks. | Cards |
| Fri | **Graduation reading show** + **Final Milestone check**. Give a homemade certificate. | Checklist |

**Interference watch:** Thai has no final consonant clusters, so speakers usually reduce them to one consonant ("hand" becomes "han", "fast" becomes "fas") ([PASAA systematic review](https://digital.car.chula.ac.th/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1754&context=pasaa)).

## Thai-English Pronunciation Pitfalls

Thai allows only **eight final consonant sounds** and **no final clusters**, so English endings are the biggest challenge ([PASAA systematic review](https://digital.car.chula.ac.th/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1754&context=pasaa)). Thai also has only three fricatives (/f/, /s/, /h/), while English has nine ([PASAA](https://digital.car.chula.ac.th/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1754&context=pasaa)).

| Pitfall | What you'll hear | Example | Home fix |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Unreleased or dropped final stops** | The end of the word disappears | "cat" → "ca'", "book" → "boo'" | Hand-puff test. "Make the end pop." |
| **Final voiced sounds become voiceless** | d → t, g → k, z → s, v → f | "bad" → "bat", "dog" → "dok" | Hand on throat: "Is your motor on?" |
| **Final /l/ becomes /n/** | Words ending in l sound like n | "ball" → "ban", "bell" → "ben" | Lollipop tongue: keep the tip up at the end |
| **Final /s/ dropped** | Plurals and verbs lose their -s | "two cat", "he run" | Snake tail at the end. One-vs-many game |
| **/v/ becomes /w/ (start) or /f/, /p/ (end)** | "vet" → "wet", "five" → "fife" | | Teeth on lip + buzz. Mirror check |
| **/z/ becomes /s/** | No buzz | "zoo" → "soo", "is" → "iss" | Bee vs snake. Throat buzz |
| **/sh/ becomes /ch/** | "ship" → "chip", "fish" → "fitch" | | Long smooth "shhh" (sleeping baby) |
| **/th/ becomes /t/, /d/, /s/ or /f/** | "thin" → "tin", "this" → "dis", "with" → "wit" | | Tongue peeks out. Mirror check |
| **/r/ and /l/ confusion** | r said as l, or dropped | "red" → "led", "frog" → "fog" | Motorbike /r/ (no tongue touch) vs lollipop /l/ |
| **Extra vowel in clusters** | A vowel added between consonants | "stop" → "sa-top", "blue" → "ba-lue" | Glued pegs: two sounds, one push |
| **Voiced /g/ and /j/ unvoiced** | "go" sounds like Thai ก, "jam" like จ | | Gulp for g, jelly wobble for j, with throat buzz |
| **Vowel length / tone carry-over** | Short vowels stretched, or Thai tones added to English words | "sit" → "seat", rising tone on a word end | Clap short vowels quickly. Speak in a flat, natural "robot" voice for practice |
| **/e/ vs /a/** | "bed" and "bad" sound the same | "pen" → "pan" | Mirror: "a" opens the mouth wider (big smile + drop jaw) |
| **Adding "uh" to letter sounds** | "suh-a-tuh" | Blending fails | Pure sounds. Model them carefully yourself |

These patterns are well documented in Thai learners. Research reviews highlight /v/, /θ/, /ð/, /z/, /ʃ/ and /ʒ/ as especially difficult because they don't exist in Thai ([Thai journal study on consonant challenges](https://so05.tci-thaijo.org/index.php/tgt/article/view/273223)). Common substitutions include /z/ → [s], /v/ → [w] and /θ, ð/ → [t, d] ([JEL Journal](https://so17.tci-thaijo.org/index.php/JEL/article/download/2385/1502)).

**A realistic expectation:** At six, Iris is at a great age for building new sounds, but /th/, /v/ and /z/ may take months beyond this plan. Count it as success when she knows the sounds are different and can make them in slow, careful speech. She doesn't need to get them right in every fast sentence yet.

### Milestone Checks

**Milestone 1 (End of Week 4)**
- ☐ Knows 16+ sounds (s a t p i n m d g o c k ck e u r)
- ☐ Blends 10 CVC words like sat, pin, dog, red, cup
- ☐ Makes final /t/ and /p/ audible in at least half of words
- ☐ Enjoys sessions (if not, shorten them and add more games)

**Milestone 2 (End of Week 8)**
- ☐ Knows all 26 letter sounds
- ☐ Reads 20 mixed CVC words with fewer than 4 mistakes
- ☐ Can show the mouth difference for v/w and s/z, even if not perfect
- ☐ Reads 2 short sentences with tricky words

**Final Milestone (End of Week 12)**
- ☐ Reads digraph words (ship, chip, thin, ring)
- ☐ Reads words with starting and ending blends (stop, frog, hand, jump)
- ☐ Says plural endings in most words
- ☐ Reads a 5-sentence mini story made from known words
- ☐ Writes 5 dictated words with correct sounds

## Tips for Success

- **Short is better than long.** Stop at 15 minutes even if things are going well, so Iris wants more tomorrow.
- **If a week feels hard, repeat it.** It's better to spend two weeks on Week 4 than to rush.
- **Read aloud daily, separately from phonics.** Simple English picture books (with you reading) build vocabulary. Phonics only works when Iris knows what the words mean.
- **Use her world.** Make words from things she loves: "cat", "bus", "pink", "fish", "mango" (later).
- **Check for hearing problems.** If Iris struggles to hear any sound differences after several weeks, a quick hearing check is worth doing.
- **After Week 12:** Move on to long vowel patterns (ai, ee, igh, oa, oo), which is *Letters and Sounds* Phase 3, and keep the Friday interference tracker going.
